Prompt and rubric errors
A task fails when someone can honestly do it two different ways, or when the rubric scores something other
than the work the prompt asked for. This page shows those mistakes in plain language, with a bad line and a
good line for each.
The counts come from 340 prompt reviews and 343 rubric reviews. 104 prompts failed. 130 rubrics failed. A
score of 1 or 2 on any requirement fails the task. The task’s score is the lowest score it got.
First issue. The checks that catch the prompt and rubric fails in those reviews, with a bad line and a
good line for each.
Rewritten in plain language. Same rules as the October 4 issue.
The clock now means your universe date: October 1, 2025 or February 14, 2026.
Combined with the Unique Ground Truth guide: section 2 now covers what fails and what does not,
the five checks a reviewer uses, and when more than one answer is acceptable. Section 6.1 has nine
more patterns.
Updates
Go through sections 2 to 5 in order. If you cannot answer one, stop and fix the task before you continue. An empty
answer means the task can still fail that check. Sections 6 and 7 are examples, for a check you are stuck on.

6.8 Clock, labels, broken data, and SQL

already happened in May 2027 is a 3, which blocks a perfect score and does not fail the task. A future deadline can

Snowflake and Confluence. A closed ticket that still has later activity, or line items that do not add up to the total,

7.9 Weights, process rows, and rows that always fail
Weight
such as , e.g. , and for example in a title or note that gets scored are a moderate issue, once per row.
A process row is only for an order that every valid path must follow, and that a result row cannot see. Most tasks
need none. Two invalid process rows fail the rubric.
Two rows that every careful attempt will fail, because the value is not in the records or only another person can see
it, fail the rubric before anyone runs the task. An agent missing a fact that is actually there is a different problem.
Weight the work in the row. A row does not get a 5 because it feels important. A row that packs two writes gets
split, and each piece is weighted on its own.
